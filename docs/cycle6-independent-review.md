### Crown judgment

**Suitable for root promotion as an incremental improvement**, provided its independent technical gates pass. No newly observed whole-vehicle silhouette regression requires rejecting the volume gain. It should remain an open crown iteration.

The crown-completion blockers are the narrow/tubular distribution of its strongest curvature, the abrupt forward transition, and the graphic's heavy forward tongue. Recheck and blend these transitions before adding more crown height. The front grille/bumper/valance and overall hood-to-cabin impression also remain visibly simplified, so this promotion cannot establish the front or whole car as matched.

## Dashboard: four fresh comparisons

1. **Druk 140 with second-pass passenger paint/clay, checked against the original before.** Re-examined `build/dash-volume-cycle5/references/druk-140.jpg`, before/after `dashboard-passenger.png` and after-clay `dashboard-passenger.png`. The second pass adds a passenger upper pad, inner rectangular vent, a shaped lower bright line and a curved pull, replacing the earlier plain box. These additions address visible shared construction. The real pad is one sculpted, textured mass with a deep rounded central shoulder. The candidate still separates into an extruded driver cap and a passenger box with an abrupt junction; that join remains a major identity defect.
2. **Druk 107 with front/quarter paint and the original front baseline.** Re-examined `druk-107.jpg`, before/after `dashboard-front.png` and after `dashboard-quarter.png`. The rolled driver brow gives the candidate useful depth and is preferable to the shallow original outline. Yet the metal driver fascia remains a near-rectangular board with uniformly arranged square pockets. The reference surround sweeps with the brow and has more varied inset depth, lower curvature and control hierarchy. The second pass improves the housing while leaving most of this dominant face unresolved.
3. **Druk 109 with the second-pass quarter/front and clay quarter.** Re-examined `druk-109.jpg`, after `dashboard-quarter.png`, `dashboard-front.png` and after-clay `dashboard-quarter.png`. The original slotted metal three-spoke wheel and padded boss continue to convey the appropriate construction, and the dash brow does not depend on silver texture alone. Fine vent vanes, the control ledge, radio/climate stack and their relative recesses still read as simplified rectangles/lines. The photo's clock/automatic equipment is not evidence to replace the target's unresolved dial or manual controls.
4. **All three Druk views against the whole cabin, then the passenger/front closeups again.** Re-examined 140/107/109 together with after `whole-cabin.png`, `dashboard-passenger.png` and `dashboard-front.png`. The improved pad and passenger vent are legible within the cabin and the extra volume is not merely closeup decoration. The separate cap ends, rectangular driver field and simplified center/lower assembly remain evident at cabin scale. The bright lower trim also reads as a thin isolated line rather than the reference's seated continuous molding with a clear center turn. The cutaway captures intentionally omit exterior occlusion, so they do not prove native occupant visibility or complete assembly fit.

### Dashboard judgment

**Suitable for root promotion as an incremental improvement**, subject to root's combined clearance and visibility gates. It is a better starting point than the original flat passenger/driver boxes. It does not establish an overall dashboard match, and the available images do not justify closing the section.

The main dashboard-completion blocker is the overall integrated shell: shape the driver brow's right turn and passenger central shoulder as one convincing hierarchy, then refine the driver fascia perimeter and lower stack/trim. Avoid making the cap simply thicker or rounder, which would exaggerate the separate tubular pieces. Resolve the target's dial/equipment evidence before adopting supplemental detail.
