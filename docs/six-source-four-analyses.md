## Analysis 1: independent complete review

**B:** Listing text and the 73-photo set cover body, glass, grille, four rear lamp pods, spoiler, cabin, engine, underside and damaged areas. Smooth glazing surrounds, inset bright handles and deep lamp bezels are useful. The conspicuous bad door gap agrees with the seller's poor-fit statement and must not become our fit target. Its faded cabin cannot establish fresh stripe colours. Current model handles and bumper facets are visibly simplified.

**D:** Text and all 201 entries establish fuller front crowns, rounded rear shoulders, thin window chrome, rounded quarter-window corners, contoured spoiler and rear bumper. Interior coverage includes front buckets, rear bench, both door cards, headliner and cockpit. Actual dash and compliance-label photographs agree on `A3C798H187455`, whereas prose says `A3C797X039279`. Preserve that contradiction. Vinyl roof, automatic console and conventional rear exhaust remain this example's equipment. The model's sail, window corners and spoiler are more angular.

**M:** Complete article and 14 photographs show a long muscular body surrounding a compact cabin, raised front crowns and a real roof/backlight lip. The 1972 SST's grille and full-width rear lighting do not define the target's 1973 AMX face. Cabin photographs corroborate the striped door insert and cushion layout but do not establish a complete headliner. Historical numbers remain editorial claims pending primary literature.

**F1:** Every post and all 22 owner photographs show shell, hood repair, exposed front architecture, engine assembly and installation. The owner explicitly describes a fiberglass replacement trunk. Missing glass, trim and upholstery make this a construction reference with no finished-cabin or final-fit authority. Watermarks further limit fine-detail reading. Its unfinished stance is unsuitable for proportion matching.

**F5:** Full posts and four March collages show completed exterior, four rear pods, dashboard and console. September images were initially missed because separate HTTP retrieval failed; this review's coverage was completed with the 29 recovered browser photographs (addendum below). Configurations must remain chronological. Watermarks and indoor reflections limit fine colour and dimension judgments. Painted bumpers and altered signals are not target instructions.

**H:** Article, correction comments and inventoried image slots show close-up hood seams, crowns, quarter swage, chrome junctions, handle recess, rear lamp bevels and upholstery. Corrected 1973 identity takes precedence over stale URL text. Preserve the real roof-edge lip while refining its transition into the sail. The current model instead reads as intersecting planes. Rectangular signals and rack are variant equipment. Editorial design criticisms do not authorize redesigning AMC's shapes.

**Recovered F5 addendum:** Reviewer 1 independently inspected all 29 September photos and enlarged exterior/profile/upholstery views. These show rectangular grille lamps, painted bumpers, hood pins, vinyl roof, rally wheels and rear exhaust, distinct from target equipment. Rounded crowns, continuous crease, shoulder, roof lip, spoiler, chrome framing and four rear pods are corroborated. Cabin includes engine-turned fascia, manual shifter, armrest, curved backrest bands and straight cushion stripes; headliner remains insufficiently shown in this set. The earlier circular-lamp observation was limited to March collages.

**Pass limits:** Exterior model renders support a shape critique, not a current cabin certification or measured/native alignment claim. Source discrepancies remain explicit. F5 recovered-photo addendum completes its coverage without changing the initial access record.

## Analysis 2: independent complete re-examination

**B:** Complete listing and 97-entry set resolve the curved quarter-window termination, narrowing rear glass, four rear lamps with inner reverse inserts, raised spoiler and hood T extending across fixed fender ends. Upholstery images distinguish nested backrest ribbons from straight cushion stripes. Explicit damage, poor fit and missing front spoiler disqualify those features as ideal geometry. Modified powertrain and wheels do not define target equipment.

**D:** All 201 entries show the front crown rising independently above the hood edge and descending toward cowl/door, with a convex door below a strong longitudinal crease. Rear views show bumper end wraps and central plate relief. Open-panel and cabin photos clarify thickness, padded seats and sculpted dash depth. Certification photo reads November 1972 and VIN `A3C798H187455`; body plate separately reads body `039279`, model `7379-8`, trim `341W`, paint `D7`. These observations do not prove why the sales VIN differs. Current broad flat surfaces and simple handles remain weak.

**M:** All 14 images and complete article support raised fenders, broad rear volume entering the sail and recessed/concave rear glazing beneath a roof lip. Profile is useful for relative landmarks. The 1972 SST's woodgrain fascia and rear lighting remain unsuitable target details. Article option prices, production totals and specific mechanical claims require their own factory verification. Different restored paint and lighting prevent colour transfer.

**F1:** All posts and 22 owner photographs distinguish stripped shell, open front structure and rebuilt engine. The owner places wiring, windows, vinyl top and upholstery in future work. Thus this page establishes architecture without showing finished cabin, fitted glass or final ride height. Aftermarket intake, traction bars and fiberglass trunk belong to the restoration history, not universal AMX specification.

**F5:** Full text, four March collages and all 29 September images cover body from both sides, door openings, glass placement, rear shoulder, front crown, hood T, cabin, wheel, shifter and upholstery. SH100304/321 are useful front-crown views; SH100296/322 show the rear shoulder; SH100323 resolves padded seats and separate stripe layouts. Painted bumpers, rectangular front signals, hood pins and vinyl roof differ from the target. This is the later state of F1's project.

**H:** Complete article/comments and 71 relevant car photos resolve sculpted crowns, thin bright trim, curved roof/sail junctions, recessed handle and bevelled rear lamps. Cabin close-ups expose fabric weave and padded stripe registration. Standard grille, rack and absence of the target's AMX spoiler remain variant limits. The author's correction establishes the article's intended 1973 identity, not independent factory authentication of every fitted component.

**Pass limits:** Current quarter-window facets, straight spoiler wall, hard bumper sections and broad front lower band deserve geometry review. Studio chrome appearance may reflect lighting. Camera matching is required before numeric reshaping; photographs do not certify real-world dimensions or game alignment. Preserve the close-fitting sidepipes.

## Analysis 3: independent challenge review

**B:** Full listing and broad car-image set support continuous roof/glass contours, broad crown volumes, inset bright handles and shaped rear trim. The text's repaired panels and poor door fit explain why visually prominent seams cannot serve as ideal panel-registration evidence. Its mechanical state and weathered upholstery do not define the restored target. Model concerns remain shape-related even with those exclusions.

**D:** Full sales material and 201-entry gallery establish curved side volumes, rounded brightwork, sculpted seats and dashboard, and rear bench construction. Photographed VIN agrees across two labels while sales text disagrees; neither engine identity nor originality should be inferred from the erroneous-looking text field. Automatic controls, roof covering, wheels and rear exhaust are specific to D. Current inset-handle and sail/quarter geometry need closer work.

**M:** Complete gallery/article confirm shared underlying body architecture and characteristic roof lip but show a 1972 SST configuration. Woodgrain dashboard, grille and rear lamps cannot be substituted. Photographs support curvature and cushion/door stripe relationships; they do not establish target paint or unseen surfaces. The model's broad identity is recognizable while its short surface facets remain apparent.

**F1:** Posts and 22 watermarked construction images show an unfinished, modified restoration. Exposed shell and engine components are useful assembly references. Fiberglass trunk and missing finished glazing/interior constrain conclusions about final exterior fit, spoiler construction and Cardin installation. Owner specification statements are recorded as claims rather than factory proof.

**F5:** Four collages and all 29 recovered September photos were independently examined, with enlarged rear-quarter, low-side, cockpit and seat views. Rear shoulder is convex under the sail; low-side view shows width variation and recessed handle; seats have thick sculpted backrests, rounded corners, nested bands and straight cushion stripes. Cockpit shows engine-turned fascia, rounded instrument recesses, slotted spokes, pushbutton radio and black manual knob. Keep the target's white knob. Painted bumpers and rectangular front signals demonstrate restoration-specific differences even on a car described as AMX.

**H:** Full article/correction and relevant image set provide unusually strong close-ups of handle, window surround, roof lip, tail lamps and patterned upholstery. Rounded forms coexist with sharp styling creases. Historical fashion/other-car pictures and unrelated recommendations were excluded from vehicle geometry evidence. The corrected year and standard-car equipment must remain explicit. Opinion about changing original styling is not an accuracy criterion.

**Pass limits:** Surface relationships are strongly visible; colours, originality and exact measurements are less certain. Neither the photographed restoration nor current exterior-only renders prove target cabin conformity. This independent review agrees with the need to refine the model's shoulder, handle and window surfaces without undoing the pipes.

## Analysis 4: primary verification and reconciliation

**B:** Read listing and reviewed broad contact sheets spanning exterior, interior, engine and underside. The long hood, separate front crown and compact cabin recur across angles. Seller-reported poor door fit, damage and replacement panels make some panel gaps counterexamples rather than targets. Hood T spans both hood and fixed fender surfaces; geometry and decal registration must be checked together later. This page cannot settle factory dimensions or the target's exact components.

**D:** Reviewed listing evidence, full-gallery coverage, enlarged identification and exterior/interior contact sheets. The photographed compliance label is legible: November 1972, `A3C798H187455`. This strengthens the earlier metadata-only conflict record. The rear bench is structurally continuous, while upholstery motifs are surface-specific and include paired rounded compositions. The old phrase 'one continuous broad composition' was too imprecise and has been corrected in the earlier report. Four similar independent model buckets remain a structural mismatch with the supplemental cabin. Its exact replacement must follow target rear-seat frames.

**M:** Complete gallery/contact sheet and linked article establish the real roof lip and flowing shoulder, without making every roof transition uniformly smooth. Keep the 1972 SST's fascia, rear lighting and woodgrain trim separate. Profile supports landmark comparisons, not uncalibrated millimetre corrections. Current model shelf-like highlights and sail planes differ visibly, but camera mismatch prevents a claimed dimensional solution.

**F1:** Full captured text and all owner-photo contact coverage show the early restoration's unfinished shell and assembly sequence. A replacement fiberglass lid limits factory deck/spoiler conclusions. Missing final components cannot be treated as design absences. It is the same project as F5 and supplies no independent corroboration of final upholstery or original trim.

**F5:** Full captured posts, four owner collages and all 29 recovered native photographs were reviewed. Later photographs add usable front-crown, rear-shoulder, window, seat and dash evidence that the initial blocked image retrieval lacked. March and September stages remain distinct. Rectangular signals and painted bumpers reinforce the need to avoid merging this restoration's equipment with the red target. The unrelated member-signature vehicle was excluded.

**H:** Article/comments and all image-slot contact sheets were examined, separating car photos from historical/related content. Close-ups distinguish a recessed handle assembly with paddle/lock from our projecting block, and genuine sharp seams from soft surrounding volumes. Rear upholstery coverage corrects the earlier oversimplified pattern description. The roof lip is real; the current angular sail blend is still a concern. Rack and standard signals remain excluded from target decisions. Colour and reflectance remain lighting-dependent.

**Pass limits:** Current side/rear renders were compared with these references; exterior view opacity prevents a fresh interior-parity judgment. Current generator and retained interior evidence already establish four similar seats and generic cockpit forms, but this is not a new native inspection. No fresh video-frame comparison was completed in this task, so supplemental evidence does not authorize an exact target rebuild by itself.
